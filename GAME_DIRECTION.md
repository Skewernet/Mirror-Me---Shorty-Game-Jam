# MIRROR ME — direction and performance script

Mara / picture and story pass, in development. Original setting, characters, dialogue and assets. This document contains the full puzzle route and ending. The checked-in game events and `src/story.mjs` are the source of truth; final playthrough results belong in `PROJECT_STATUS.md` and `DEVLOG.md`.

## The story the player discovers

Mara Vale has come back to collect her mother's belongings. She asked for one light to be left on. She expects boxes, an empty bedroom and a task she has been putting off. Instead, the telephone rings with a request she used to hear every evening: fix the kitchen light, finish the routine, stay until Mum is asleep.

The house remembers Mara through the things she did for someone else. The kitchen radio kept Mum company. Running the bath meant the evening was nearly over. The bedroom music box meant she could sleep. Mara continued those gestures after the hospital called at 03:17, as if stopping would be another way of leaving her mother. The diary admits this before Mara can say it aloud.

The reflection learns the same routine. It first reproduces Mara's movements, then supplies what she is avoiding: the key, a familiar accusation, an offer to take over. Its final claim is intimate rather than supernatural: “I can stay with her. You couldn't.” It knows how to sound like a dutiful daughter. That does not establish whether it understands either woman.

The player should finish with a concrete memory of what happened and an unresolved question about what occupies the house. Do not explain a demon, diagnose Mara, claim that grief caused the haunting, or reveal which caller was “really” speaking. The second caller sounds like Mara; the game does not certify that it is her future self. The hospital letter, the diary and the late reflection should allow the player to assemble the relationship without a final exposition speech.

## INVERTED as a progression

1. **Movement:** an ordinary reflection becomes late, then stops copying Mara.
2. **Habit:** a learned evening must be undone in reverse, rather than completed again.
3. **Space and image:** a familiar furnished house develops local reversals of orientation, color and light. Recognizable objects carry the changes.
4. **Responsibility:** the copy offers to stay where Mara could not. Its apparently helpful offer becomes an accusation.
5. **Freedom:** the reflection acquires a body that can leave the glass. Mara retains the view but loses the ability to follow.

Take structural inspiration from P.T.: make a domestic route legible, change it outside the player's attention, and let the same ordinary sound acquire a different meaning on return. Use original rooms, imagery, performances and events. Repetition should produce new evidence, not simply add walking time.

## Mara and the visual language

Mara is an adult woman in ordinary clothes: a burgundy cardigan over a light blouse, dark trousers, practical shoes, a small pendant and shoulder-length brown hair. Her silhouette and clothes remain consistent in the faithful reflection, passage sighting and replacement. The player needs to recognize the same person before any distortion has weight.

Start with a recently occupied home: worn floral paper, timber, yellowed linen, stained tile, rain on the windows, a telephone table, the hospital envelope and objects placed for habitual use. Kitchen and bedroom dressing should suggest particular routines. Keep the kitchen doorway readable before its overhead light is restored. Warm practical lamps can leave shadow between them, but neither an important door nor an interaction should depend on the flashlight to become discoverable.

Smooth geometry, credible materials and contact shading carry the picture. Optional low internal resolution is a presentation choice. It does not excuse a featureless face, flat furniture, clipped cloth or unreadable black areas. Preserve crisp text, quiet grain and optional head motion.

The reflection's later damage should retain Mara's identity: wrong gaze and timing first, pallor and asymmetry next, then restrained blood-like staining around an eye or mouth. Avoid covering the face with an anonymous monster mask. The player should think “that is me, but something has happened to her.” The real player view does not need a matching wound.

## The house and its sightlines

The entry opens along a short wing. Turning into the long hall produces an L-shaped approach with the mirror directly ahead on the north wall. This is a physical composition; no automatic camera turn is needed. The hall clock occupies the left wall near that end. A return passage behind the central partition connects the north turn to the same entry, forming one continuous house.

The sitting room and bathroom open off the main hall. Kitchen, bedroom and utility room open off the return passage. The small brass key opens the bedroom. The front door is the eventual exit, not a second lock for that key. The return circuit advances an authored pass only after its current discoveries are complete; incomplete circuits retain progress.

Five passes remain the scope. Measure the new route after the layout change. The previous 232-second known-solution test is not a length measurement for this revision, and an 8–12 minute first-time target remains a playtest goal. Do not pad the route with mandatory idle waits.

The faithful-mirror gate uses about 1.3 seconds of attention. The delayed encounter arms after about 2.1 seconds, then requires a nearby look-away and about 1.3 seconds of observation on return. The finale requires facing the occupant and moving away for about 1.6 seconds after gaining distance. These short observation windows establish intention; they are not theatrical pauses that must be stretched to make the game longer.

## Environmental escalation for this pass

The existing photograph, breathing quilt, ceiling chair, emptied mirror and watcher form the backbone. The house now includes room-specific dressing, a frame that falls on the return-passage approach in pass two, a cup that slides and falls on a later kitchen approach, and localized negative-color surfaces. Collision-respecting browser validation checks the triggered falls, inversion and full reset; publication remains a separate approval.

Use the following staging rules for those additions:

- Establish a picture, cup or coat as an ordinary object before moving it. The same placement should be reliable on replay until its authored trigger.
- Let one thing happen, give it a physical source and leave time to inspect the result. A falling frame should produce an impact from the frame's location. A cup moving in the kitchen should sound like ceramic in that room.
- Make local inversion recognizable: a section of familiar wallpaper becomes a photographic negative, a practical lamp changes its warm/cold relationship, or an object reverses orientation while its neighbors stay ordinary. Avoid a full-screen color flash.
- Keep passage width and door visibility intact. Fallen decoration must not become an accidental progression lock.
- Preserve the quiet earned by the reverse-sound puzzle through the final pass. Do not restart the tap or radio as incidental ambience after the player has silenced them.

## Written discoveries

The folded note remains concise: **“IF THE HALL RETURNS, WATCH THE GLASS. WHEN IT LEADS, YOU LEAVE.”** It begins as a way to test the reflection and ends as a reason to refuse it. Mara recognizes the handwriting; do not name an offscreen author to resolve that question.

The hospital envelope is optional, marked **ST. ANNE'S — PERSONAL EFFECTS — E. VALE — collected by MARA VALE**. The journal records the watch among the returned possessions and Mara's refusal to open its bag. It makes the loss concrete without requiring the player to read it to progress. The 03:17 clue still requires discovering the bathroom writing in the current progression; an early correct clock setting must recover safely once the clue is found.

The diary names actual rooms and objects, not a code word for a different prop: kitchen radio for company, Mum's bath, bedroom music box. Its confession is that Mara continued the evening after the nurse called. It explicitly asks her to end the routine in reverse. The journal preserves this information and distinguishes the two callers from the three sounds.

Do not add another mandatory collectible, keypad cipher or narrated backstory to make the story feel larger. Let the room support the text: personal effects left half-collected, two settings at the kitchen table, a bedroom kept ready, a light deliberately left on.

## Performance and sound implementation

Mara and the glass share a recognizable woman’s voice. Mara hesitates and corrects herself; the glass spends no effort on those corrections. It should not sound like a different monster. The first telephone voice should suggest Mum; the second should suggest Mara. Until directed acting is available, the current synthetic rendering can establish timing and different acoustic treatment but cannot stand in for a fully directed two-character performance.

All current takes are **synthetic provisional performances**, rendered offline from the public-domain LJ Speech dataset model. No human acting is claimed. Player, glass, telephone and radio treatments remain reproducible in the generation tool. Human replacement recording brief: two restrained takes per story line, 48 kHz mono WAV, no baked music/reverb, and two seconds of room tone. Record Mum and Mara as distinct character performances with permission; do not imitate an identifiable actor.

Narrative dialogue belongs in the foreground. The radio bulletin is sparse ambience; mute it with voice playback if that is the selected setting. Its words are not needed to solve a puzzle. Keep the telephone ring separate from radio static, the water grounded in the bathroom, and the music box in the bedroom. Voices and essential environmental responses should remain intelligible at the default volume. Sound begins after a gesture and suspends during pause, journal and clock inspection.

Silence is an authored event. The final routine should remove the radio, water and music box rather than reward each action with a musical sting. A wrong-order response can imply that the evening has started again, but it must not punish experimentation with a loud transient or permanent failure.
